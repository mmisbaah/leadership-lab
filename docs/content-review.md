## Session 01: Leadership foundations

*SELF · 60 minutes · Activity: Leadership Survival Kit*

Leadership is a pattern of choices and behaviours. Begin with the influence you have, the trust you create, and the responsibilities you accept.

**Outcomes**

- Distinguish leadership from position and authority.
- Identify behaviours that make a leader credible.
- Choose one leadership behaviour to strengthen.

**Model:** Notice the situation → Choose a fair response → Explain the purpose

**Activity: Leadership Survival Kit**

1. Form teams of four to six.
2. Select five behaviours a new leader needs most.
3. Rank them and defend the top two with a workplace example.
4. Debrief how values become visible in daily decisions.

**Decision quiz**

A capable team member makes an error in front of others. What is the strongest first response?

- A. Correct them publicly so everyone learns.
- B. Protect dignity, establish facts, and address the error privately. ✔
- C. Ignore it to preserve morale.

*Why:* A private, factual correction protects dignity while keeping accountability. Follow up on any immediate operational risk first.

**Notes prompt (private to each participant):** What is one behaviour people can consistently expect from you as a leader?

#### 12. Three steps for leading in the moment · Flow

*The model*

- **Notice the situation**: Pause long enough to see what is really happening.
- **Choose a fair response**: Pick the action that is right, not just the fastest.
- **Explain the purpose**: Tell people why, so they can act well without you.

> **Speaker notes:** Introduce the three steps. Say it takes seconds, not minutes, once it is a habit. The next three slides take each step in turn.

#### 13. Notice the situation · Points

*Step 1*

- Separate what you saw from what you assume
- Check who is affected and how
- Ask one question before you react
- Notice your own mood: are you tired, rushed, irritated?

> **Speaker notes:** Most leadership mistakes come from reacting to a story we told ourselves. Give an example: a late arrival assumed lazy turns out to be caring for a sick parent.

#### 14. Choose a fair response · Points

*Step 2*

- Would I respond this way to anyone?
- Does it protect dignity?
- Does it keep the standard?
- Could I defend it in public?

> **Speaker notes:** These four questions are a quick fairness test. The last one is powerful: if you would be uncomfortable explaining it to the whole team, think again. | Full wording from the earlier slide: On-screen points: Would I respond the same way to anyone on the team?; Does it protect people’s dignity?; Does it keep the standard?; Can I defend it if everyone hears about it?

*Step 3*

- Link it to the goal the team cares about

> **Speaker notes:** People who know the purpose can make good decisions when you are not there. People who only know the instruction cannot.

## Session 02: Functional leadership: task, team and individual

*SELF · 45 minutes · Activity: Three Circles Audit*

Every leader balances three overlapping needs: achieving the task, building and maintaining the team, and meeting the needs of each individual. Neglect one and the other two suffer.

**Outcomes**

- Explain the three areas of functional leadership: task, team and individual.
- Use the leadership check sheet to plan, brief, control, support, inform and evaluate.
- Spot which need is being neglected and restore the balance.

**Model:** Achieve the task → Build and maintain the team → Develop each individual

**Activity: Three Circles Audit**

1. Give each group a short case about a team that is struggling.
2. Map what the leader is doing for the task, the team and the individuals.
3. Identify the neglected area and its knock-on effects on the other two.
4. Use the check sheet to write the leader’s next three actions, then share them.

**Decision quiz**

Your team meets every deadline, but two experienced members have asked to transfer out. What is most likely being neglected?

- A. The task: the deadlines are not ambitious enough.
- B. Team and individual needs: workload, recognition and development. ✔
- C. Nothing: results are all that matter.

*Why:* Strong results can hide a team that is wearing out. The three needs overlap: neglect people for long enough and the task suffers too.

**Notes prompt (private to each participant):** Which of the three areas do you tend to neglect, and what one action would rebalance it this week?

## Session 03: Followership: the ten rules

*SELF · 40 minutes · Activity: Ten Rules Case Clinic*

Most leaders are also followers. Good followership means supporting decisions once they are made, disagreeing in private and with evidence, keeping your leader informed and taking responsibility without waiting to be told.

**Outcomes**

- Explain why effective followership matters to a team and its leader.

## Self-assessment (before and after)

Rated 1 (Rarely) to 5 (Consistently) at Session 01 and again at the 30-day check-in. Facilitators see class averages only, when at least 3 people answered.

1. I give specific feedback within a week of seeing the behaviour.
2. When I ask for something, I explain why, not just what.
3. I listen to the end before I answer or decide.
4. People on my team tell me about problems early, including my own mistakes.
5. I pay attention to the task, the team and each individual, not just the task.
6. When I delegate, I agree the outcome, the limits and when we will check in.
7. I spend time helping each person grow, not only fixing today’s work.
8. I explain how a decision will be made before we make it.
9. I deal with disagreement directly and early instead of letting it build.
10. When I disagree with my own leader, I say so in private, with evidence, then support the decision.
